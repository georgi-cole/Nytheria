Science & privacy

Evidence, context and honest uncertainty.

Precision health works only when good science, individual context, professional judgement and data protection are handled together.

Evidence principles

Commercial confidence without scientific theatre.

01 / Relevance

Evidence before novelty.

A new test or intervention is not automatically useful because it has molecular vocabulary and a premium price.

02 / Context

Patterns before isolated values.

Preparation, temporary illness, medication, lifestyle and medical history can change what a result means.

03 / Probability

Predisposition, not destiny.

Genetic and ageing signals describe probabilities or patterns, not a complete forecast of one person’s future.

04 / Boundaries

Referral before overreach.

Findings that require diagnosis or treatment belong in appropriate medical care, not inside a wellness slogan.

Biological age

Different clocks measure different biology.

Biological-age methods may use DNA methylation, glycan patterns, routine clinical biomarkers or other molecular and physiological features. They can differ in purpose, sensitivity, repeatability and response to short-term change.

Nytheria will publish the selected method, why it was chosen, its validation context, sample requirements, expected variability and limitations before offering the test.

A result should be treated as a method-specific signal within a wider assessment, not a definitive age, diagnosis or promise of lifespan.
01
What is measured?

The molecular or clinical features behind the score.

02
What is the intended use?

Baseline description, risk research, change tracking or another defined purpose.

03
What counts as change?

Analytical and biological variability must be considered before celebrating decimals.

04
What can it not tell you?

No clock can replace a medical history, examination or multi-system view.

Genetics

Your genome is context, not a sentence.

Genetic information can help identify inherited predispositions, inform conversations about screening or explain why some responses differ. Most complex health outcomes reflect interactions among many variants, environment, behaviour, age and chance.

The final genetic scope must define which variants or traits are included, who interprets them, whether confirmatory testing is needed and how incidental or family-relevant information is handled.

See the genetics role

Consent must be specific.

People should understand what is tested, what is not tested, who can access the result, how long data are kept and whether the information may have implications for relatives.

Recommendation governance

From correlation to a responsible next step.

Every recommendation should pass through an explicit filter rather than emerging from a colourful dashboard with mysterious authority.

01

Evidence

What supports the action, for which population and with what uncertainty?

02

Relevance

Does the member’s context make the recommendation appropriate?

03

Safety

Could medication, disease, pregnancy, deficiency, excess or another factor change the risk?

04

Measurability

What outcome will show whether the action helped, harmed or did nothing?

Privacy from the start

Health and genetic data require more than a padlock icon.

This public website collects contact information only. Clinical intake and genetic data will use a separate secure process with clear consent, access and retention rules.

Data minimisation

Collect only what is necessary for a defined purpose.

Informed consent

Explain processing, choices and consequences in understandable Bulgarian and English.

Controlled access

Role-based access, logging and clear professional responsibilities.

Defined retention

State how long data are kept and why.

Secure partners

Laboratories and technology providers must be contractually and technically reviewed.

Rights and deletion

Provide practical routes for access, correction, objection and deletion where applicable.

Data controller, lawful bases, hosting location, processors, security controls, retention periods, clinical records policy and the final privacy notice remain to be approved before launch.
Selected scientific framework

Sources will be published, not merely implied.

The final methodology page will cite the evidence behind each included assessment and recommendation. The following works illustrate the broader framework, not a validated Nytheria protocol.

Ageing biology

Hallmarks of Aging

López-Otín and colleagues’ updated framework organises major mechanisms of ageing while also showing why no single marker captures the entire process.

Geroscience

Ageing and chronic disease

The geroscience framework examines shared biological processes linking ageing with multiple chronic conditions.

Comparative biology

Evolution-tested mechanisms

KoleQuant’s research direction reviews how long-lived and regenerative species can inform hypotheses while separating biological interest from clinical readiness.

No scientific citation, partner logo, laboratory accreditation or methodology claim will be added until it is verified and relevant to the actual service.
Limitations

What Nytheria must never promise.

Diagnosis, emergency care or treatment

Nytheria is not a replacement for licensed medical diagnosis, emergency care or specialist treatment.

Guaranteed prevention or longer life

No biomarker programme can guarantee disease prevention, reverse ageing or add a specified number of years to life.

Deterministic genetic prediction

Most complex outcomes cannot be predicted with certainty from selected genetic variants.

A perfect universal “optimal range”

Reference ranges, decision thresholds and individual targets serve different purposes and require professional context.

Responsible launch

Trust should be designed before the first sample is collected.
